A. Dissertation Proposal Format (Chapters 1–3)
The proposal establishes your research design and theoretical foundation. It’s typically 40–60 pages and includes:
Front Matter
Title Page (per Antioch template)
Abstract (150–250 words, concise summary of the study)
Table of Contents
List of Figures and Tables (if applicable)
Chapter 1: Introduction
Purpose: Establish the study’s background, problem, and significance.
Typical structure:
Introduction to the Study
Background of the Problem
Statement of the Problem
Purpose of the Study
Research Questions / Objectives
Theoretical or Conceptual Framework
Significance of the Study
Assumptions, Limitations, and Delimitations
Definitions of Key Terms
Summary
Chapter 2: Literature Review
Purpose: Establish scholarly context and theoretical grounding.
Typical structure:
Introduction
Overview of Major Theoretical and Conceptual Frameworks
Review of Empirical Studies Related to the Topic
Gaps and Synthesis Leading to the Study
Conceptual Model or Integrative Framework (if used)
Summary and Transition
Chapter 3: Research Design and Methodology
Purpose: Present the detailed design and rationale for your approach.
Typical structure:
Introduction
Research Design (e.g., Design Science Research + Modified Delphi Study)
Research Context and Setting
Participants or Expert Panel (if applicable)
Data Collection Methods
Data Analysis Procedures
Validity, Reliability, and Trustworthiness
Ethical Considerations and IRB
Summary and Transition
📘 B. Full Dissertation Format (Chapters 1–5 or 6)
After proposal approval and research completion, the full dissertation integrates findings, interpretation, and implications.
Chapter 4: Findings / Results
Purpose: Present analyzed data and key findings.
Typical structure:
Introduction
Data Preparation and Analysis Overview
Presentation of Results (aligned to each research question)
Summary of Key Findings
(For Design Science Research or innovation studies, this may include the artifact design, prototype evaluation, and Delphi consensus outcomes.)
Chapter 5: Discussion and Interpretation
Purpose: Interpret findings within theoretical and practical context.
Typical structure:
Introduction
Interpretation of Key Findings
Relationship to Theoretical Framework and Literature
Implications for Practice, Policy, and Future Research
Limitations of the Study
Summary
Chapter 6 (if required): Conclusions and Recommendations
(Some Antioch committees combine this with Chapter 5, others require it as a standalone.)
Typical structure:
Summary of the Study
Conclusions Drawn from Findings
Practical Recommendations
Directions for Future Research
Final Reflections
Back Matter
References (APA 7th edition, double-spaced, hanging indent)
Appendices (IRB materials, instruments, correspondence, prototypes, visual models, Delphi materials, etc.)
🧾 Formatting Notes
Typeface: 12-point Times New Roman (or approved equivalent)
Margins: 1 inch on all sides
Line Spacing: Double-spaced throughout (except tables, long quotes)
Page Numbers: Bottom center, no “running head”
Citation Style: APA 7th edition (strictly enforced)
Binding Order: Title Page → Abstract → Acknowledgments (optional) → TOC → Chapters → References → Appendices
