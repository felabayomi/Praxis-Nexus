Intersectionality, Queer/Quare Theory, and My Dissertation

The intersectionality chapter frames intersectionality not just as a buzzword, but as a Black feminist methodology for naming how interlocking systems of oppression shape people’s lives and life chances. Haynes, Stewart, and Patton argue that without an intersectional lens, the most vulnerable lives—especially those of Black women and girls—are misread, erased, or blamed for the harms they experience. That pushes me to see my human–wildlife conflict app not as a neutral tool, but as something that will sit inside these same power structures.

For my dissertation, an intersectional approach means I cannot imagine a single generic “user.” Risk and access will differ by race, gender, class, age, geography, and migration status. Some communities may face greater danger from wildlife and have the least access to smartphones, data, or trusted authorities. Intersectionality methodology insists on asking whose risks are being centered, whose knowledge counts as data, and whose safety and liberation the tool is ultimately serving. Those questions can shape both my expert panel composition and the criteria I use to evaluate the prototype.

Alexander’s queer/quare chapter adds another important layer: queer/quare theory is about resisting “regimes of the normal” and exposing how norms are produced and enforced. In the context of my app, this challenges me to question seemingly simple binaries like safe/unsafe, legal/illegal, “problem” animal or “problem” community. The way the app classifies incidents, suggests “proper” behavior, or encodes who is a legitimate reporter can easily reinscribe dominant views while marginalizing others.

Together, intersectionality and queer/quare theory encourage me to treat the prototype as more than a technical fix. They invite me to design and evaluate it in a way that resists erasure, makes room for complex identities and experiences, and remains suspicious of any design choice that quietly normalizes one group’s perspective as universal.
