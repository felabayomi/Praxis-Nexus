Month 2: February — Exploring and Looking Closely

February feels less like trying to prove something and more like trying to look around carefully. I am not trying to win an argument. I am trying to notice things.

This month is about slowing down and really seeing how AI is used in real life. I want to see how it connects to money, rules, the environment, and systems we don’t always see.

As I try different tools and read about real examples, I am asking myself: What do I see now that I didn’t see before?

What good things and problems did I notice?

One big good thing is that AI can look at a lot of environmental information very quickly. It can help track animals, guess where conflicts might happen, and see how land is changing. This can help people make better decisions and act earlier to protect nature.

But there are also problems.

AI needs a lot of energy and big machines to work. Building and running these systems can also harm the environment. Even if AI seems fast and smart, it still costs money and resources. It also depends on who is paying for it and what they care about.

So AI is not just a simple “solution.” It is connected to money, politics, and power. The people with money often decide which problems matter most.

What surprised or worried me?

What worries me is how people talk about technology like it is something we must accept. They often say, “This is the future,” without asking if it is the best choice.

I also noticed that people don’t talk much about the hidden costs. They don’t always mention the energy used, the materials taken from the Earth, or the land needed for big data centers. These costs are real, but they are often ignored.

On a personal level, I feel two things at once.
I am curious and excited about what AI can show us about nature.
But I am also careful and a little worried. Technology can change who has power and who makes decisions. If we are not careful, it can create new problems.

What patterns am I seeing?

I am noticing that technology often follows money. It is built where there is already a lot of money. It is not always built where nature needs the most help.

I also see that AI does not fix unfair systems. It usually makes them stronger. If power is unfair already, AI can make it even more unfair. But if people share power and work together, AI might help them become stronger and more prepared.

So AI itself is not good or bad. It depends on the people, the rules, and the systems around it.

Where I Am Right Now

Right now, I am not trying to give final answers. I am trying to understand things more deeply.

My main question is becoming clearer, but also more complex. Before I can talk about how AI affects animals, land, or people’s jobs, I need to understand where the money comes from and how the systems are built.

For me, February is about learning patiently.
I am practicing staying curious without rushing to solve everything.

The real joy right now is not about new technology.
It is about seeing patterns, noticing problems, and letting my questions grow stronger before I try to answer them.
